Common Plumbing Problems in Berkley Square

Berkley Square has a mix of older homes and commercial buildings, and that creates specific plumbing problems. Hard water, mature trees, and cold winters all take a toll. Here are the issues we see most often and how we fix them.

  • Sewer line root intrusion

    Berkley Square has mature trees, and their roots seek out moisture in sewer lines. Older clay and cast iron pipes have joints that roots can penetrate. Once inside, roots grow and catch debris, causing backups.

  • Frozen pipes in winter

    Berkley Square winters drop below freezing, and pipes in unheated crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls are at risk. When water freezes, it expands and cracks the pipe, causing a leak when it thaws.

  • Water heater failure from sediment

    Berkley Square water has high mineral content. Over time, sediment builds up in the bottom of the tank, causing overheating, popping sounds, and eventually a leak. The anode rod also corrodes, leading to tank failure.

  • Low water pressure from corroded pipes

    Older homes in Berkley Square may have galvanized steel pipes that corrode from the inside. The rust restricts flow, so you get low pressure at every fixture. It gets worse over time.

Seasonal Plumbing Considerations in Berkley Square

Berkley Square sees all four seasons, and each one brings different plumbing concerns. Winter freezes pipes, spring rains fill basements, summer stresses water heaters, and fall is the time to prepare. Here is what to expect and what you can do.

  • Spring

    • Spring rains and snowmelt can overwhelm sump pumps and cause basement flooding. Sewer lines also back up more often because the ground is saturated and roots are growing.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit. If it does not kick on, call us. Also schedule a sewer camera inspection if you had backups last year.

  • Summer

    • Summer is hard on water heaters and outdoor plumbing. Higher water demand and warmer temperatures can push an aging water heater to failure. Sprinkler systems and hose bibs can also leak.
    • Flush your water heater to remove sediment. Check outdoor faucets for leaks and make sure the shut-off valves inside work. If your water heater is over 10 years old, start budgeting for a replacement.

  • Fall

    • Fall is the time to prepare for winter. Pipes in unheated spaces are at risk, and sump pumps need to be ready for spring. Leaves and debris can also clog gutters and drains.
    • Insulate exposed pipes in your garage, crawl space, and attic. Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor faucets. Schedule a plumbing maintenance visit to check everything before the cold hits.

  • Winter

    • Freezing temperatures are the main threat. Pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces can freeze and burst, causing major water damage. Water heaters also work harder in winter.
    • Keep your thermostat at 55 degrees or higher, even when you are away. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to let warm air circulate. If a pipe freezes, call us before you try to thaw it yourself.
