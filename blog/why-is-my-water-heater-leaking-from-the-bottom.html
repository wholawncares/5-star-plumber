Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom

A puddle under your tank rarely means the tank itself failed. Here are the real causes of a water heater leaking from the bottom in Arlington Heights IL, what repairs cost in 2026, and when replacement makes more sense.

Five Star Plumber LLCNovember 20269 min read

A water heater leaking from the bottom is almost always one of five things: a loose drain valve, a failed T&P relief valve, a leaking inlet or outlet nipple, condensation from a cold tank, or a rusted-through tank. Four of those are repairable. Only the last one means the tank is finished. If the water is pooling directly under the middle of the tank and the shell feels soft or damp to the touch, stop reading and shut the gas or breaker off - that one is a replacement.

We run these calls across Arlington Heights every week, and the diagnosis usually takes under ten minutes. What follows is how we tell those five causes apart, what a fix costs in 2026, and when a repair stops making sense.

The short answer: where the water is actually coming from

Water heater leaking from bottom Arlington Heights IL is a plumbing fault where water escapes at or below the tank base, and it is most often a $150 to $350 fitting repair rather than a $1,800 tank replacement. That distinction matters, because homeowners frequently replace a perfectly good heater over a $12 drain valve.

Water runs downhill. A leak at the top of the tank - a corroded nipple, a loose union, a dripping relief valve - travels down the shell and pools at the base. By the time you see the puddle, the source can be three feet above it. That is why we never quote a replacement over the phone. We look first.

Here is the rough split we see on service calls in the 60004 and 60005 ZIP codes:

  • Drain valve or T&P valve: about 40% of bottom-leak calls
  • Inlet/outlet nipple or union: about 25%
  • Condensation (gas units, cold weather): about 15%
  • Rusted tank shell or base: about 20% - the only category that requires replacement

Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom in Arlington Heights IL?

Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom in Arlington Heights, Illinois comes down to the age and water chemistry of the unit sitting in your basement or utility closet. Most Arlington Heights homes were built between 1955 and 1995, and the original heaters are long gone - but the plumbing around them often is not. Galvanized nipples, original gate valves, and undersized drain pans are still common in the older blocks near downtown and along the Northwest Highway corridor.

1. A failed drain valve

The drain valve sits at the bottom of every tank, and it is the single most common source of a bottom leak. The plastic or brass spout cracks, the washer behind it dries out, or sediment jams the seat open. You will usually see a slow drip at the spout itself, or a wet ring right at the base of the valve.

Replacing a drain valve is a 45-minute job. We shut the water off, drain the tank, swap the valve, and refill. Typical cost: $150 to $250.

2. A leaking T&P relief valve

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a safety device. It opens when tank pressure exceeds 150 PSI or water temperature hits 210°F. If it drips constantly, either the valve has failed or your system pressure is genuinely too high. That second possibility is the one people ignore, and it is the one that leads to tank failure.

We test incoming pressure at the same visit. Arlington Heights municipal water typically runs 55 to 75 PSI. Anything above 80 PSI needs a pressure-reducing valve, and that is a separate repair.

3. Corroded inlet or outlet nipples

The nipples are the short pipes threading into the top of the tank. On older units they are galvanized steel and they rust from the inside out. Water weeps down the shell and shows up at the bottom. If you see rust streaks on the side of the tank, trace them upward - the source is almost always at the top.

4. Condensation, not a leak

Gas water heaters in a cold basement pull moisture out of the air. In January and February, when basement air drops to 45°F and the tank holds 120°F water, condensation forms on the shell and drips to the floor. It looks exactly like a leak. If the moisture is a thin film rather than a steady drip, and it disappears after the first ten minutes of a burn cycle, you have condensation, not a failure.

5. A rusted-through tank

This is the one that ends the unit's life. Steel tanks have a glass-lined interior and a sacrificial anode rod. Once the anode is consumed - usually at the five-to-seven-year mark - the lining starts to fail and the steel rusts through. Water then seeps through the shell itself. You cannot patch it. No epoxy, no sealant, no weld holds. The tank is done.

If your heater is past ten years old and the bottom is wet with no visible fitting leak, assume tank failure until proven otherwise. Our water heater repair Arlington Heights IL team confirms it with a pressure test and a visual inspection of the base before we recommend anything.

How to tell a tank failure from a fitting leak in five minutes

You can narrow this down yourself before anyone arrives. Here is the sequence we walk homeowners through on the phone.

  1. Shut the water off. Close the cold inlet valve on top of the tank. If the leak stops within a few minutes, the source is on the supply side - a fitting, a valve, or a nipple.
  2. Dry the base completely. Towel it dry and watch. A tank failure re-wets the base within 60 seconds even with the water off, because the tank still holds 40 to 50 gallons.
  3. Check the T&P discharge pipe. If it is wet or dripping, the relief valve is the source. That is a $180 to $300 repair.
  4. Look for rust streaks. Vertical rust lines on the shell mean the leak started above the water line.
  5. Check the age sticker. Every tank has a serial number that encodes the manufacture date. If the unit is over 10 years old and the base is wet, plan for replacement.

If you are on a slab and the heater sits in a closet with no drain pan, water spreads fast. That is when we get calls for emergency Plumbing Arlington Heights IL - usually at 11 p.m. with water heading toward a hallway. Shut the inlet valve first, then call.

Common mistakes we see on water heater leak calls

The mistakes that turn a $200 repair into a $2,000 water-damage claim are almost always the same three.

Ignoring the leak because it is slow

A drip at one drop per second wastes about 3,000 gallons a year. More importantly, it keeps the tank base wet, which accelerates rust on the shell and the fittings. A slow drip becomes a fast failure.

Sealing the outside of the tank

Epoxy putty and tank sealant do not work on a pressurized vessel. They hold for a week, then fail under 60 PSI. If the shell is rusted through, no external product fixes it.

Replacing the heater without checking system pressure

If your home runs at 95 PSI and you install a new tank without a pressure-reducing valve, the new unit fails early too. We check incoming pressure on every replacement. It takes two minutes and it saves the next heater.

One more: never cap or plug a T&P valve to stop a drip. It is a safety device, and disabling it is a code violation and a genuine scald risk.

Should you repair or replace a leaking water heater?

Repair the heater if the tank is under eight years old and the leak is at a valve, nipple, or union. Replace it if the tank is over ten years old, the shell is rusted, or the base is wet with no visible fitting leak.

Three questions settle it:

  1. How old is the tank? Under 8 years, repair. Over 10, replace. Between 8 and 10, it depends on the cause.
  2. Where is the water coming from? A fitting is a repair. The shell is a replacement.
  3. How many repairs in the last two years? Two or more, and you are funding a replacement one repair at a time.

If you are replacing, this is also the moment to consider a tankless unit. A tankless water heater services Arlington Heights IL install eliminates the tank-failure risk entirely and typically lasts 20 years instead of 10. It costs more up front and needs an annual descaling in our water hardness range, but it removes the puddle problem for good.

When to call a professional

Call us when the water is still flowing after you close the inlet valve, when you smell gas near the heater, or when the leak is anywhere near electrical outlets or a finished floor. Gas connections, T&P valves, and pressure-reducing valves are not DIY work - a mistake on any of them is a safety issue, not a plumbing inconvenience.
