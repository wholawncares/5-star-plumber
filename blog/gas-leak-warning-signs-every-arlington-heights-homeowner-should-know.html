Gas leak warning signs every Arlington Heights homeowner should know

Sulfur odor, hissing gas lines, dead grass, and higher bills are the gas leak warning signs Arlington Heights homeowners should never ignore. Here's what to do.

Five Star Plumber LLCNovember 20269 min read

The most common gas leak warning signs Arlington Heights IL homeowners report are a rotten-egg smell, a hissing sound near a gas appliance, dead patches of grass over a buried line, and physical symptoms like headaches or dizziness that ease when you leave the house. If you notice any of these, get everyone outside and call 911 or Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 from a neighbor's phone before you do anything else. This guide walks through each warning sign, how to check safely, and what our team does when we arrive.

Natural gas itself has no odor. Utilities add mercaptan, a sulfur-based compound, so leaks are detectable at concentrations as low as 1 part per million. That is why the smell is so reliable and why ignoring it is never the right call.

What are the gas leak warning signs Arlington Heights IL homeowners should watch for?

Gas leak warning signs Arlington Heights IL residents should treat as urgent include smell, sound, sight, and physical symptoms, and any one of them is enough to act on. You do not need all four. A single hiss behind a stove or one dead shrub over the service line is a reason to clear the house.

Here is the short list we walk through with every homeowner who calls us about a possible leak:

  • Smell: sulfur, rotten eggs, or a skunk-like odor near the meter, furnace, water heater, or stove.
  • Sound: a hiss, whistle, or roar near a gas line or appliance, even when nothing is running.
  • Sight: dead grass in a strip, bubbling water in the yard, dust blowing from a crack, or a flame that burns orange instead of blue.
  • Feel: headaches, nausea, dizziness, or eye irritation that fade when you step outside.
  • Money: a gas bill that jumps 20% or more with no change in usage.

The smell of sulfur is the number one sign

The sulfur smell is the single most reported indicator, and it is the one that sends people outside fastest. Mercaptan is added at the city gate, so any natural gas leak signs Arlington Heights IL homeowners detect by nose are already at a concentration worth taking seriously.

Where you smell it matters. A faint whiff only near the water heater usually points to a pilot or thermocouple issue. A smell that is strong in the basement, near the meter, or throughout the first floor suggests a supply-line leak, and that is a different level of response.

One caution: some people lose the ability to smell mercaptan after a few minutes of exposure, a condition called olfactory fatigue. If you smelled gas, stepped away, and came back to nothing, do not assume the leak stopped. Your nose simply adjusted.

What should I do if I smell gas in Arlington Heights, Illinois?

If you smell gas in Arlington Heights, Illinois, leave the building immediately, do not touch light switches or use your phone indoors, and call 911 or Nicor Gas from outside or from a neighbor's home. Every second you spend investigating indoors is a second you spend inside a potentially explosive atmosphere.

Follow this order:

  1. Get everyone, including pets, out through the nearest door. Do not stop to open windows.
  2. Do not flip switches, unplug anything, or use a garage door opener. A spark from any of those can ignite gas.
  3. Once you are at least 100 feet away, call 911 and Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748.
  4. Wait for the utility or fire department to clear the building before you go back in.
  5. After the utility caps the line, call a licensed plumber to find and fix the source.

Physical symptoms that point to a natural gas leak

Gas leak symptoms Arlington Heights, Illinois residents describe most often are headaches, nausea, fatigue, and dizziness that improve within 20 to 30 minutes of leaving the house. That pattern, better outside, worse inside, is the tell.

Long-term exposure at low levels is the sneaky version. A small leak behind a furnace or in a crawlspace can run for weeks, and the symptoms look like seasonal allergies or a bad night's sleep. If two or more people in the home report the same symptoms on the same days, that is a pattern worth investigating.

Carbon monoxide is a separate hazard with similar symptoms, and it comes from incomplete combustion rather than a raw gas leak. If your CO alarm is going off, treat it the same way: out of the house, then call 911.

How to check for a gas leak in Arlington Heights IL

How to check for a gas leak Arlington Heights IL homeowners can do safely is limited to a soapy-water test on an accessible connection, and even that only when you already know where to look. Anything beyond that, including crawlspaces, meter fittings, and buried lines, belongs to a licensed technician.

The soapy-water test

Mix one teaspoon of dish soap in a cup of water. Brush it on the joint you suspect, usually where the flexible connector meets the shutoff valve behind a stove or water heater. If bubbles grow, gas is escaping. If nothing bubbles, that does not prove the line is tight, only that this one fitting is not the source.

What not to do

Never use a lighter or match to check for a leak. Never use an open flame to test a gas appliance. And never trust a $15 hardware-store gas detector for a decision about your family's safety. Those units false-alarm on paint fumes and miss low-level leaks.

Professional detection uses a combustible gas detector calibrated to methane, plus a manometer to check line pressure at the meter. We also use a combustible gas indicator on every joint from the meter to the appliance, and we document the readings before and after the repair. For homes with older galvanized or polybutylene supply lines, we often pair that with a video camera inspection Arlington Heights IL to look inside the run before we commit to a repair method.

Common mistakes homeowners make during a gas leak

Mistakes during a suspected gas leak turn a manageable repair into a dangerous event. The pattern we see most often is homeowners trying to be helpful before they get out.

  • Calling from inside the house. A phone, even a cell phone, can spark. Call from outside.
  • Opening windows to air it out. That delays evacuation and does not lower the risk enough to matter.
  • Shutting off the gas at the meter without training. The valve can seize, and a partially closed valve is worse than an open one.
  • Relighting a pilot to "test" the line. That is how houses catch fire.
  • Waiting until morning. Gas leaks do not respect business hours. Treat every smell as a now problem.

One more: do not assume the utility will fix the leak. Nicor Gas will make the scene safe and cap the line, but the repair on your side of the meter is the homeowner's responsibility. That is where a licensed plumber comes in.
