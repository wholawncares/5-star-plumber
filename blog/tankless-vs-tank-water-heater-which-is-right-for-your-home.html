Tankless vs tank water heater: which is right for your home

Tankless vs tank water heater in Arlington Heights: compare upfront cost, lifespan, and hot-water output to pick the right unit for your 60004 home.

Five Star Plumber LLCNovember 20269 min read

For most Arlington Heights homes with two or more bathrooms and a family of four, a tankless water heater is the better long-term choice - but a tank unit still wins on upfront price and simple swap-out installation. The right answer depends on your household size, your gas line capacity, and how long you plan to stay in the house. Below we break down real 2026 costs, lifespan numbers, and the specific quirks of heating water in 60004 so you can decide with actual numbers instead of sales talk.

The short answer: tankless vs tank water heater in Arlington Heights

Tankless vs tank water heater Arlington Heights IL is really a question about how your household uses hot water, not which technology is newer. A tankless unit heats water only when you open a tap, so you never pay to keep 40 or 50 gallons hot in a basement all day. A tank unit stores hot water around the clock and delivers it instantly, but it runs out after the stored volume is gone.

Here is the honest split for local homes:

  • Choose tankless if you have three or more people, two-plus bathrooms, and you plan to stay in the house five years or longer.
  • Choose tank if you need the lowest upfront cost, your gas line is undersized, or you are selling the home within a couple of years.
  • Either works for one- and two-person households where showers rarely overlap.

For a broader look at how we handle hot-water calls across the village, our Plumbing Arlington Heights IL team runs both tank and tankless installs every week.

How each system actually works in an Arlington Heights home

A tank water heater is a glass-lined steel cylinder that holds 40 to 80 gallons of water at roughly 120 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit, reheating it as heat escapes through the tank walls. A tankless water heater is a wall-mounted heat exchanger that fires a gas burner only when water flows, heating it to your set temperature in about 5 to 10 seconds.

That difference matters in Arlington Heights because most of the housing stock here was built between the 1950s and the 1990s. Older basements have 1/2-inch gas lines that were sized for a 40-gallon tank, not a 199,000 BTU tankless unit. If the gas line is too small, the tankless heater short-cycles and you get lukewarm water at the far shower. We check gas line size, venting, and water hardness before quoting any tankless job - those three items decide whether the install is a clean swap or a bigger project.

Water hardness is the other local factor. Arlington Heights draws Lake Michigan water, which runs moderately hard at roughly 7 to 9 grains per gallon. That scale builds up inside tankless heat exchangers and needs an annual descaling flush to keep efficiency up. Tank units handle hardness differently - the scale settles at the bottom of the tank and shortens its life instead.

Tank vs tankless cost in Arlington Heights IL: what you pay in 2026

Tank vs tankless cost Arlington Heights IL breaks into three numbers: the unit, the installation, and the operating cost over 10 years. Typical 2026 pricing in this area looks like this:

  • Tank water heater, 40-50 gallon gas: $1,200-$2,200 installed, including haul-away of the old unit and a new expansion tank.
  • Tankless water heater, whole-home gas: $3,200-$5,500 installed, depending on whether we need to upsize the gas line or add a dedicated vent.
  • Annual operating cost: a tank unit runs roughly $350-$500 per year in gas; a tankless unit runs about $180-$300 for the same household.

Add it up and the tankless unit usually pays back the price difference in six to nine years through lower gas bills - sooner if your household uses a lot of hot water. One cost people forget: tankless units need an annual descaling service, roughly $150-$250 per year in this area. Skip it and the heat exchanger clogs, which is the single most common reason a tankless heater stops keeping up.

Tankless water heater pros and cons Arlington Heights homeowners should weigh

Tankless water heater pros and cons Arlington Heights homeowners ask about usually come down to endless hot water versus higher upfront cost. Here is the straight list.

Pros

  • Endless hot water - you can run the dishwasher, two showers, and the laundry without the third tap going cold.
  • Lower gas bills, typically 20 to 35 percent less than a tank for the same usage.
  • 20-year service life versus 8 to 12 years for a tank.
  • Frees up roughly 9 square feet of basement floor space.

Cons

  • Higher upfront cost, often two to three times a tank replacement.
  • Needs a 3/4-inch gas line and a dedicated vent in most Arlington Heights basements.
  • Requires annual descaling because of local water hardness.
  • Cold-water sandwich - a few seconds of cool water when you first turn on a tap after a gap.

If your existing tank is leaking or your basement floor is wet, that is a different problem. A failing tank usually shows up as rusty water, popping sounds, or a puddle under the unit, and our water heater repair Arlington Heights IL team handles those calls same-day before the leak reaches your stored boxes.

Which water heater is better Arlington Heights for your household size?

Which water heater is better Arlington Heights depends almost entirely on how many people shower back-to-back. Use this as a rough guide:

  1. 1-2 people, one bathroom: a 40-gallon tank is fine and the cheapest path.
  2. 3-4 people, two bathrooms: tankless starts to win on both comfort and long-term cost.
  3. 5+ people or a large soaking tub: tankless with a recirculation loop, or a 75-gallon tank if you want to stay with storage.

If you are unsure, we size the unit based on your peak-hour demand - how many fixtures run at once and how many gallons per minute they pull. A 199,000 BTU tankless unit handles about 11 gallons per minute at a 35-degree temperature rise, which covers most Arlington Heights homes with two showers and a dishwasher running together.

For homes with a finished basement or a bathroom addition, we often pair the water heater work with bathroom Plumbing services Arlington Heights IL so the new fixture and the hot-water supply are sized together instead of fighting each other.

What to watch out for before you switch

The biggest mistake we see is buying a tankless unit online and then discovering the gas line, vent, and electrical requirements do not match the house. A few things to check before you commit:

  • Gas meter capacity. A tankless unit pulls 150,000-199,000 BTU at full fire. Some older meters in Arlington Heights need an upgrade from Nicor before the install can pass inspection.
  • Vent termination. Tankless units vent through PVC, but the termination has to meet Illinois code for distance from windows, doors, and air intakes.
  • Condensate line. High-efficiency tankless units produce acidic condensate that needs a neutralizer and a drain path.
  • Freeze protection. Northern Illinois winters drop below zero. A tankless unit in an unheated garage or crawl space needs freeze protection or it will crack the heat exchanger.
  • Water softener pairing. If your water is hard, a softener upstream of the tankless unit can double the time between descaling services.

We also see homeowners skip the permit. Arlington Heights requires a permit for water heater replacement, and an unpermitted install can stall a home sale years later. We pull the permit and handle the inspection as part of the job.

When to call a professional

Call us when your tank is leaking from the bottom, when your hot water runs out in under five minutes, or when you are ready to price a tankless upgrade and want a real number instead of an online estimate. Gas connections, venting, and code compliance are not DIY work in Illinois - a mistake here is a carbon monoxide risk, not just a plumbing inconvenience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is tankless better than tank in Arlington Heights IL?

For households of three or more people, yes - tankless delivers endless hot water and cuts gas use by 20 to 35 percent. For one- and two-person homes, a 40-gallon tank is cheaper upfront and works fine. The deciding factors are household size and how long you plan to stay.

How much does a tankless water heater cost in Arlington Heights, Illinois?

Whole-home tankless installs run $3,200-$5,500 in 2026, including the unit, venting, and permit. If your gas line needs upsizing, add $400-$900. Annual descaling service runs $150-$250 because of local water hardness.

How long do tank water heaters last in Arlington Heights IL?

Expect 8 to 12 years for a gas tank unit here. Lake Michigan water runs 7 to 9 grains per gallon hard, so scale builds up on the tank bottom and shortens life. Annual flushing gets you closer to the 12-year end of that range.

Should I switch to tankless in Arlington Heights?

Switch if you have two or more bathrooms, three-plus people, and plan to stay five years or longer - the gas savings pay back the price gap in six to nine years. Stay with a tank if you need the lowest upfront cost or your gas meter cannot support a 199,000 BTU unit.

Do tankless water heaters work in cold Illinois winters?

Yes, as long as the unit is installed indoors or has freeze protection. Northern Illinois winters drop below zero, so a tankless heater in an unheated garage or crawl space needs a freeze-protection kit or the heat exchanger can crack.
