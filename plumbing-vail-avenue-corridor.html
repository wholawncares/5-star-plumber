Common Plumbing Problems in Vail Avenue Corridor

The corridor's building stock and climate drive specific problems. Older commercial buildings with historic downtown infrastructure see more drain and shut-off failures, while winter cold brings frozen pipes. Hard water in Cook County adds scale to water heaters and fixtures.

  • Grease and scale buildup in commercial kitchen lines

    Restaurants in the corridor produce grease that coats pipe walls and narrows the line over time. Older cast iron lines make it worse because the rough surface holds debris.

  • Frozen pipes in exterior walls

    Older buildings often have pipes in exterior walls with little insulation. A sustained cold snap can freeze them, and a thaw can cause a burst pipe.

  • Failed shut-off valves in mixed-use buildings

    Valves that are never exercised seize over time. When a repair requires a shut-off, the valve will not close, and water has to be shut at the main.

  • Sump pump failure during spring rain

    Pumps sit idle for months and fail when they are needed. A stuck float or a burned motor can leave a basement vulnerable.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Vail Avenue Corridor

Winter is the season that drives the most emergency calls in the corridor, but each season brings its own plumbing concerns. Planning ahead reduces the chance of a 2 a.m. call.

  • Spring

    • Spring rain tests sump pumps and sewer lines. Basements in older buildings can take on water if a pump fails or a line is root-intruded.
    • Test your sump pump before the first heavy rain and have your sewer line camera-inspected if you have had backups.

  • Summer

    • Commercial kitchen drains see heavy use, and water heaters work harder. Backflow testing season is in full swing for businesses with irrigation systems.
    • Schedule drain maintenance and backflow testing before the summer rush so you are not caught without a record.

  • Fall

    • Falling temperatures mean it is time to winterize. Exterior hose bibs, exposed pipes, and older heating systems need attention before the first freeze.
    • Disconnect hoses, shut off exterior valves, and have us check any pipes in unheated spaces.

  • Winter

    • Frozen pipes and burst pipe calls spike. Water heater failures also increase because the incoming water is colder and the unit works harder.
    • Keep the heat on in vacant units and call us immediately if you see a pipe freeze or a water heater leak.
