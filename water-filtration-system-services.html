What Our Filtration Service Includes

Every job includes the water test, the system sizing, the installation, and a walkthrough of how to run and maintain the equipment. Add-ons like softeners, UV stages, and backflow assemblies are quoted separately after the test, so you only pay for what your water needs.

  • On-Site Water Testing

    We pull a sample from your kitchen cold line and test for hardness, chlorine, iron, and total dissolved solids. The reading takes about 15 minutes and gives us the numbers to size the system correctly instead of guessing from your address.

  • System Sizing and Quote

    We match flow rate to your household size and fixture count, then give you a flat written price. You will know the equipment model, the installation time, and the cartridge cost before we start any work.

  • Filter Replacement Plan

    We set a reminder based on your actual water use, usually every 6 to 12 months for carbon and 2 to 3 years for RO membranes. When it is due, we bring the right cartridge and check the housing O-rings during the same visit.

How Our Installation Process Works

Every job follows the same five steps, whether it is a single under-sink unit or a whole-house system with a softener. You will know what is happening and when before we start cutting pipe.

  1. Water Test

    We pull a sample from your kitchen cold line and run it through a field test kit for hardness, chlorine, iron, and total dissolved solids. The reading takes about 15 minutes and tells us exactly what your water needs. If you have a private well or an older service line, we add a bacteria and lead check before recommending anything.

    • Sample pulled
    • Field test
    • Results read

  2. System Sizing

    We match the filter to your household size, fixture count, and water pressure. A family of five with three bathrooms needs a different flow rate than a two-person condo near Arlington Market. We also check your main shut-off valve and pressure gauge reading so the new equipment does not choke an already weak supply.

    • Flow rate
    • Fixture count
    • Pressure check

  3. Installation Day

    Most whole-house installs take three to five hours. We shut the main, cut in a bypass loop with copper pipe or PEX tubing, mount the housings, and pressure-test every joint. Under-sink reverse osmosis units run about two hours. We lay drop cloths, haul away packaging, and leave the work area cleaner than we found it.

    • Bypass cut in
    • Housings mounted
    • Joints soldered

  4. Testing and Flush

    After the glue and solder cure, we flush the system for 10 to 15 minutes to clear carbon fines, then re-test the water at the tap. You get a walkthrough of the bypass valve, the cartridge schedule, and what to do if you ever see a drip. Nothing gets signed off until the pressure holds steady.

    • Flush lines
    • Re-test water
    • Walkthrough given

  5. Filter Schedule

    We set you up on a replacement reminder based on your actual water use, usually every 6 to 12 months for carbon and 2 to 3 years for RO membranes. When you call, we bring the right cartridge, swap it, and check the housing O-rings for wear. That is the whole maintenance visit, and it takes under an hour.

    • Cartridge swap

    • Schedule reset

Tools and Methods We Use

Move across a circle, or focus it and press Enter, for an illustrative thermal view of the photograph.

  • Pressure Gauge

    We read static and dynamic pressure before sizing a system, because a filter that is too restrictive on a low-pressure line will starve your fixtures.

  • Pipe Cutter and Soldering Torch

    Clean cuts and proper solder joints on copper pipe are what keep a filtration tie-in from leaking behind a cabinet wall years later.

  • PEX Crimping Tool

    For PEX tubing runs, we use a calibrated crimping tool so every ring is set to spec and the connection holds at full house pressure.

  • Tube Cutter

    Reverse osmosis systems use small-diameter tubing, and a square cut with a tube cutter prevents the push-fit fittings from weeping.

  • Leak Detection Equipment

    If water appears near the new install, we use leak detection equipment to confirm whether it is a fitting, a housing O-ring, or an unrelated pipe issue.

Methods

  • Watts and RIDGID Components

    We use Watts valves and RIDGID tools because the parts are serviceable, widely stocked, and hold up to Illinois water chemistry.

  • Field Water Testing

    We test hardness, chlorine, iron, and total dissolved solids on site before recommending equipment. The numbers decide the system, not a sales script. If your water only needs a carbon block, that is what we quote.

  • Bypass Loop Installation

    Every whole-house system gets a bypass loop with shut-off valves, so you can isolate the filter for cartridge changes or repairs without losing water to the rest of the house. It also makes future service faster and cleaner.

  • Pressure Testing to 100 PSI

    After installation we pressure-test the new joints to 100 psi, well above normal household pressure, and hold it long enough to confirm nothing weeps. That step catches a bad solder joint before it becomes a cabinet flood.

  • Illinois Plumbing Code Compliance

    We follow Illinois plumbing code on every tie-in, including proper backflow protection where required. When a permit or backflow test is needed, we handle the paperwork and the test so the install passes inspection.
