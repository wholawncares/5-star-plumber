What Our Water Softener Service Includes

Every job starts with a water test and a clear scope. Installation and repair cover the equipment, the plumbing connection, the drain, and the programming. We tell you upfront what is included and what would be an add-on, like a whole-house filter or a dedicated soft water tap.

  • Water Hardness Testing

    We test hardness, iron, and chlorine at your tap and check static pressure with a pressure gauge. You get the actual numbers before we recommend anything, so the decision is based on your water, not a sales script.

  • Softener Sizing and Quote

    We calculate grain capacity from your hardness and household size, then quote the unit and labor together. You see the full price before we start, including permit and any fittings needed for the tie-in.

  • Installation and Plumbing Tie-In

    We cut into the main, run PEX tubing or copper pipe to the unit, install shut-off valves, and set the drain line with a proper air gap. All fittings are pressure tested before we close anything up.

  • Repair of Valves and Tanks

    For repairs we diagnose the control head, timer, brine valve, resin tank, and bypass assembly. We rebuild or replace the failed part and run a full regeneration cycle to confirm the fix.

  • Programming and Walkthrough

    We set hardness compensation, regeneration schedule, and brine refill, then show you how to add salt and read the display. You leave knowing how the system works and when to call us.

  • Post-Job Testing

    We retest hardness at the tap after the install or repair and check every fitting for leaks. You get the before and after numbers in writing, plus a reminder for annual service.

Tools and Methods We Use

Move across a circle, or focus it and press Enter, for an illustrative thermal view of the photograph.

  • Pipe cutter and soldering torch

    Used to cut and join copper pipe cleanly at the softener tie-in. A clean cut and proper solder joint prevent leaks at the main connection.

  • PEX crimping tool

    For PEX tubing runs, which are faster and more flexible than copper in tight mechanical rooms. The crimp rings are pressure tested before we close up.

  • Pressure gauge

    Checks static and dynamic water pressure before and after the install. High pressure above 80 psi needs a regulator to protect the softener and fixtures.

  • Hardness test kit

    Measures grains per gallon, iron, and chlorine at the tap. The numbers drive sizing and tell us whether a filter is needed alongside the softener.

  • Drain snake and auger

    Clears softener discharge lines and floor drains clogged with salt and sediment. A clear drain is required for the regeneration cycle to work.

Methods

  • Leak detection equipment

    Finds hidden supply line and slab leaks that a constantly cycling softener can mask. We repair the leak before finishing the softener work.

  • Water hardness and iron testing first

    We never quote a softener without testing. Hardness, iron, and chlorine numbers determine unit size, resin type, and whether a pre-filter is needed. Testing takes 15 minutes and prevents the most common mistake in the industry: installing the wrong size unit.

  • Code-correct drain and air gap

    Softener discharge must drain through an air gap to prevent cross-contamination of the water supply. We install the drain to Illinois Plumbing Code and test it during a full regeneration cycle before we call the job done.

  • Full regeneration cycle test

    After any install or repair we run the unit through a complete cycle, watching the brine draw, backwash, and fast rinse. That confirms the valve, timer, and drain all work together before we leave.

  • Anode rod check on water heaters

    When we install a softener near a water heater, we pull and inspect the anode rod. If it is scaled or eaten down, we replace it so the heater gets the full benefit of softened water.

Hard Water Problems We Solve in Arlington Heights

Hard water shows up in ways most people do not connect to their plumbing. Here are the problems we fix most often for Arlington Heights homeowners and businesses.

  • No regenerationWater softener not regenerating Arlington Heights ILThe unit sits full of brine and never cycles, so hardness climbs back within days. You notice spots on dishes and stiff laundry before you notice the softener is dead. We test the timer, control valve, brine line, and resin. Most no-regeneration calls are a clogged brine line or failed timer, which we repair the same visit. If the resin is exhausted, we replace the tank instead of the whole system.
  • Salt bridgeSalt bridges and mushy brine tanksA salt bridge forms a crust that keeps salt from dissolving, so the unit regenerates with plain water. The tank looks full but the water stays hard. We break up the bridge, clean the tank, and check the brine valve and float. We also show you how to check for bridging yourself by tapping the tank with a broom handle.
  • Leaking unitSoftener leaking onto the floorA leaking control head or cracked resin tank can dump gallons into a mechanical room. Left alone it damages drywall, flooring, and the water heater sitting next to it. We shut off the supply, find the leak at the head, tank, or fitting, and repair or replace the failed part. We also check the drain line and floor drain so the next cycle discharges cleanly.
  • Scaled heaterHard water scaling the water heaterScale builds on the tank and anode rod, shortening heater life and raising energy bills. A heater that should last twelve years fails at six or seven. We install or repair the softener and often replace the anode rod at the same time. Softened water feeding the heater stops new scale from forming and protects your investment.
  • Iron stainingIron staining from well-blended waterIron leaves orange stains in sinks, tubs, and laundry, and it fouls softener resin faster than hardness alone. A softener by itself will not solve it. We test for iron and add an iron filter ahead of the softener when needed. That protects the resin, stops the staining, and keeps the softener working at full capacity.
