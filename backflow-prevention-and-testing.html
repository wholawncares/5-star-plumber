See how it works

Backflow prevention and testing Arlington Heights IL is the inspection, testing, and repair of the mechanical assemblies that stop contaminated water from reversing into the clean supply. A reduced-pressure zone assembly, double check valve, or pressure vacuum breaker sits on your irrigation line, boiler feed, or fire sprinkler connection. Each device has check valves and relief ports that wear out. We test them with a calibrated gauge, record the readings, and make sure the assembly holds tight under both static and running pressure.

Arlington Heights sits on the older side of Cook County water infrastructure, and many homes near Scarsdale and Old Arlington Heights still run original irrigation lines tied into 60005 and 60004 service connections. The Illinois EPA requires annual testing on most assemblies, and the local water department tracks every report. How our backflow testing visit works

From the first phone call to the filed report, here is exactly what happens when you book backflow device testing Arlington Heights with our team.

  1. Schedule and confirm

    You call or book online and we lock in a two-hour arrival window. We ask for the assembly make, model, size, and location so the tester arrives with the right gauge and repair kit. Most residential irrigation assemblies take 30 to 45 minutes on site.

    • Call booked
    • Window set
    • Parts ready

  2. Inspect the assembly

    The tester shuts the isolation valves, checks for leaks, corrosion, and freeze damage, then confirms the device matches the serial number on file with the water department. If the assembly is buried or boxed in, we note it before testing begins.

    • Valves shut
    • Leaks checked
    • Serial matched

  3. Run the pressure test

    Using a calibrated differential pressure gauge, we test the check valves and relief port at the required PSI thresholds. A reduced-pressure zone assembly must hold a 2 PSI differential on the first check and open the relief valve at 2 PSI. We record every reading.

    • Gauge hooked
    • Checks run
    • Readings logged

  4. Repair or replace

    If the assembly fails, we quote the repair on the spot. Most failures are a worn check valve or relief port disc, which we replace in under an hour. If the body is cracked from a frozen pipe, we swap the whole assembly and retest the same visit.

    • Parts swapped
    • Assembly retested
    • Pass confirmed

  5. File the report

    We submit the backflow test report to the Arlington Heights water department and email you a copy for your records. Commercial accounts get the report tagged with the account number so your property manager has it for the compliance file.

    • Report filed
    • Copy emailed
    • Reminder set

Assembly types you will see in Arlington Heights

Most properties here have one of three assemblies, and each one tests a little differently.

Reduced pressure zone

Two check valves plus a relief port

  • Highest protection level
  • Common on irrigation
  • Relief port must open

Double check

Two check valves with no relief port

  • Used on fire lines
  • Tests both checks
  • No relief opening

Pressure vacuum breaker

One check valve plus an air inlet

  • Common on hose bibs
  • Must sit above grade
  • Freezes easily
