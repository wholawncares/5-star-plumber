Common Plumbing Problems in Arlington Heights Business District

The plumbing problems we see most in Arlington Heights Business District come from a few local realities: older commercial buildings with original pipe, hard water that scales equipment, shared laterals that collect grease, and cold winters that stress supply lines.

  • Grease blockages in shared restaurant laterals

    Multiple kitchens on the same block discharge into one lateral, and grease cools and hardens on the pipe wall. Over months, the buildup narrows the line until it backs up during a busy service.

  • Galvanized supply lines scaling shut

    Many older commercial properties in this district still have original galvanized steel supply lines. Hard water deposits build up inside the pipe over decades, restricting flow and causing brown water and pressure loss.

  • Root intrusion in older clay and cast iron laterals

    Trees near the district's older blocks send roots into the joints of clay and cast iron sewer laterals. The roots catch debris, and the line backs up or collapses over time.

  • Frozen and burst pipes during cold snaps

    Illinois winters push temperatures well below freezing, and pipes in exterior walls, unheated basements, and crawl spaces are the first to freeze. When the ice expands, the pipe splits and leaks when it thaws.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Arlington Heights Business District

Arlington Heights Business District sees four distinct seasons, and each one puts different stress on plumbing systems.

  • Spring

    • Snowmelt and spring rain push water into lower levels and foundation drains. Sump pumps run constantly, and older sewer laterals that collected debris over winter start backing up.
    • Test your sump pump with a bucket of water before the first heavy rain, and have your main drain jetted if it has been more than a year.

  • Summer

    • Restaurants and retail in the district run at full volume, which means more water through kitchens and restrooms. Grease lines load up faster, and water heaters work harder to keep up with demand.
    • Schedule grease line jetting and water heater service before your busiest weeks, not during them.

  • Fall

    • Temperatures drop and buildings shift from cooling to heating. This is when we inspect exposed pipes, check shut-off valves, and service water heaters before the first freeze.
    • Have your water heater flushed and your exterior pipes insulated before the first hard freeze, and test backflow assemblies before the village testing window.

  • Winter

    • Below-freezing temperatures freeze pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces, and the thaw that follows reveals splits and leaks. Water heater failures also spike because the incoming water is colder and the unit works harder.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during cold snaps, and call us immediately if you see frost on a pipe or hear water running with no fixture on.
