What our tankless water heater services include

Every job starts with a full assessment and ends with a tested, permitted installation. Here is what is covered in a standard tankless water heater service call. Add-ons like gas line upsizing or water softener installation are quoted separately before we begin.

  • Unit sizing and load calculation

    We measure incoming water temperature, count your fixtures, and calculate peak flow demand. That tells us whether you need a 7 GPM or 11 GPM unit.

  • Permit and inspection coordination

    We pull the plumbing permit from the Village of Arlington Heights and schedule the final inspection. You do not have to call anyone or fill out forms. We meet the inspector on site and correct anything they flag.

  • Gas line and venting work

    Most tankless installs need a larger gas line and a new stainless steel vent. We run the gas line, install the vent, and seal the wall penetration. All work is pressure-tested and checked for leaks before we fire the unit.

  • Descaling and maintenance

    For existing units, we flush the heat exchanger with a descaling solution and a recirculating pump. We clean the inlet filter, test the pressure relief valve, and check the anode rod. This service takes about 90 minutes.

  • Repair and parts replacement

    We diagnose no-hot-water calls, error codes, and leaks. Common repairs include flow sensors, igniters, and mixing valves. Our trucks carry parts for Navien, Rheem, and AO Smith units so most repairs finish in one visit.

Tankless water heater problems we fix in Arlington Heights

Tankless units are reliable, but they are not maintenance-free. Hard water, cold inlet temperatures, and undersized gas lines cause most of the calls we run. Here are the problems we solve every week.

  • No hot waterNo hot water or inconsistent temperatureA cold shower is annoying, but the underlying cause can be a failed igniter or a blocked flow sensor. Ignoring it leads to a full unit failure and a much larger repair bill. We test the igniter, flow sensor, and gas valve with a multimeter and pressure gauge. Most no-hot-water calls are fixed in one visit with a replacement sensor or a good cleaning of the burner assembly.
  • Scale buildupMineral scale buildup in the heat exchangerScale insulates the heat exchanger, so the unit burns more gas to heat the same water. Over time, it restricts flow and shortens the unit's life by half. We perform tankless water heater descaling Arlington Heights 60004 with a recirculating pump and manufacturer-approved descaler. The 90-minute flush restores flow and efficiency. We recommend it every 12 months.
  • Undersized gas lineGas line too small for the unitAn undersized gas line starves the burner, causing error codes and lukewarm water. It can also fail inspection, which means the unit cannot legally be used. We calculate the BTU load and upsize the gas line to 3/4-inch or 1-inch black pipe. We pull the permit, pressure-test the line, and have it inspected. The unit then fires at full capacity.
  • Condensate leakLeaking condensate line or water connectionsA slow drip under the unit corrodes the cabinet and damages the wall. In winter, a blocked condensate line can freeze and shut the unit down entirely. We replace the condensate trap and route the line to a floor drain with proper slope. We also check every water connection and replace the shut-off valve if it is seized. The area stays dry.

Condensing vs non-condensing tankless units

The two main types of tankless water heaters differ in efficiency and venting. Knowing which you have helps you understand service needs.

Condensing

Captures heat from exhaust gas for higher efficiency.

  • PVC venting
  • Efficiency above 90 percent
  • Produces condensate

Non-condensing

Vents exhaust directly without capturing extra heat.

  • Stainless steel venting
  • Efficiency 80 to 85 percent
  • No condensate line

Electric

Uses heating elements instead of a gas burner.

  • No venting needed
  • Lower flow rate
  • Higher electrical draw
