Common Plumbing Problems in Downtown Arlington Heights

The problems we see most in Downtown Arlington Heights trace back to the building stock and the climate.

  • Corroded galvanized supply lines causing low pressure

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1950s, and it rusts from the inside. The downtown housing stock has a lot of it. Over decades the pipe narrows until upstairs fixtures barely run, and the rust tints the water after a shut-off.

  • Root intrusion in older sewer laterals

    The clay and cast iron laterals under many downtown properties have joints that roots can enter. Mature trees near Evergreen Avenue and Campbell Street make it worse. Snaking clears the blockage but leaves the roots to grow back.

  • Water heater failures in tight closets

    Many downtown condos and older homes have water heaters tucked into closets that were never designed for a modern tank. Venting and clearance are tight, and the units run hard. Sediment buildup and a spent anode rod shorten their life.

  • Frozen pipes in unheated porches and crawl spaces

    Older downtown homes often have supply lines running through unheated porches, crawl spaces or exterior walls. A sustained cold snap can freeze them, and the pipe splits when it thaws. The damage usually shows up after the temperature rises.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Downtown Arlington Heights

Downtown Arlington Heights gets four distinct seasons, and each one brings its own plumbing calls. Winter is the busiest for frozen pipes and burst lines. Spring brings sump pump and sewer backup calls as the ground thaws.

  • Spring

    • As the ground thaws, sump pumps run constantly and older sewer laterals can back up from groundwater and root growth. Basements in the older housing stock are vulnerable.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float. If it does not kick on, replace it before the next heavy rain.

  • Summer

    • Water heater calls pick up as units work harder and sediment causes popping and leaks. Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation lines can fail. Commercial kitchens downtown run at full load, so grease line backups are more common.
    • Flush your water heater once a year to clear sediment. If you run a restaurant, schedule a hydro jetting visit before the busy season, not after a backup.

  • Fall

    • The first freeze is the deadline for exposed pipes. Unheated porches, crawl spaces and exterior walls are the weak points in older downtown homes. Hose bibs left connected can split the pipe behind the wall.
    • Disconnect garden hoses, shut off and drain outdoor faucets, and insulate any exposed supply line in a porch or crawl space before the first hard freeze.

  • Winter

    • Frozen pipes and burst lines are the main calls, especially during sustained cold snaps. Older homes near the Metra rail line with exposed runs are most at risk. Water heater failures also spike because units work harder in cold weather.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during a cold snap and let a trickle run overnight. If a pipe freezes, call before you try to thaw it with a torch.
