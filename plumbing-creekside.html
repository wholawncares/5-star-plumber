Common Plumbing Problems in Creekside

Creekside plumbing problems follow the local conditions: hard water that scales up fixtures, a high water table near Buffalo Creek, mature trees over older sewer lines, and a mix of copper and PEX supply piping. Understanding why these problems happen here is the first step to fixing them for good.

  • Sump pump failure during spring storms

    The water table near Buffalo Creek rises after heavy rain, and sump pumps in low-lying Creekside blocks run almost continuously during wet springs. Pumps wear out faster here than in drier areas, and a failed check valve can cause the pump to cycle itself to death.

  • Root intrusion in older sewer lines

    Mature trees along the Campbell Street corridor and older Creekside blocks send roots into clay tile and older PVC sewer lines at the joints. The roots catch paper and grease, causing repeat backups that cabled lines cannot fix permanently.

  • Scale buildup in water heaters and tankless units

    Our water is hard, and scale collects on water heater elements and inside tankless heat exchangers. A tankless unit that is not descaled annually loses capacity and eventually throws an error code. Tank water heaters lose efficiency and develop popping sounds as sediment builds up.

  • Pinhole leaks in copper supply lines

    Older Creekside homes have copper pipe that develops pinhole leaks from years of exposure to slightly aggressive water and high pressure. The leaks start small and can go unnoticed inside a wall or under a slab until the damage is significant.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Creekside

Creekside plumbing demand shifts with the seasons. Winter brings frozen pipes and water heater failures, spring brings sump pump and basement drainage calls, summer is water heater and outdoor line season, and fall is when we get ahead of winter problems.

  • Spring

    • Spring is sump pump season in Creekside. The water table near Buffalo Creek rises with the spring rain, and pumps that sat idle all winter often fail when they are needed most.
    • Test your sump pump before the first heavy rain by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float cycle. If it hesitates or runs without pumping, call us before the storm hits.

  • Summer

    • Summer brings water heater calls, outdoor hose bib leaks, and irrigation backflow testing. Hard water scale builds up faster in hot weather, and tankless units that were not descaled in spring start losing capacity.
    • Schedule a water heater flush and a tankless descale in early summer. It is a small cost that prevents a mid-July failure when you least want to deal with it.

  • Fall

    • Fall is when we get ahead of winter. We see more water heater anode rod replacements, shut-off valve checks, and pipe insulation work. Homeowners who had a slow drain all summer finally call before the holidays.
    • Disconnect garden hoses, shut off and drain outdoor spigots, and have us check the anode rod in your water heater. Those three steps prevent most winter plumbing emergencies.

  • Winter

    • Winter is frozen pipe season. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, and garages are the first to freeze, and a burst pipe can dump hundreds of gallons into your home.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during cold snaps, and let a pencil-thin stream of water run overnight when temperatures drop below zero. If a pipe freezes, call us before you try to thaw it yourself.
