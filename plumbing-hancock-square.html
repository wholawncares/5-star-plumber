Common Plumbing Problems in Hancock Square

Hancock Square has a specific set of plumbing issues that come up again and again. The mix of older commercial buildings, mature trees and hard water creates predictable problems. Knowing what to expect helps you plan and budget.

  • Sewer backups from root intrusion in older lines

    Mature trees along residential streets and the Kennicott Avenue area send roots into clay tile and older PVC joints. Once roots find a crack, they grow and block the line. Snaking clears them temporarily, but they come back.

  • Water heater failures from hard water scale

    Hard water in ZIP 60005 leaves mineral scale inside tank water heaters. The scale insulates the burner, raises energy use and shortens tank life. Anode rods deplete faster here, leaving the tank unprotected.

  • Frozen pipes during winter cold snaps

    Pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces and unheated garages are vulnerable when temperatures drop into the single digits. Older homes in Hancock Square with poor insulation are at higher risk. A burst pipe can cause thousands in water damage.

  • Backflow test failures in commercial buildings

    Commercial properties in Hancock Square with irrigation systems, fire lines or boilers need annual backflow testing. Assemblies fail when internal parts wear out or debris blocks the check valves. A failed test can lead to a citation.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Hancock Square

Hancock Square sees all four seasons, and each one brings its own plumbing concerns. Winter cold snaps, spring storms, summer irrigation demand and fall preparation all affect your pipes and equipment. Here is what to expect and what to do.

  • Spring

    • Spring storms and snowmelt put pressure on sump pumps and foundation drains. Sewer lines with root intrusion back up more often as roots wake up and grow. Basement flooding is a real risk if your sump pump fails.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit. If it does not kick on, call us. Consider a battery backup before the next storm.

  • Summer

    • Irrigation systems and outdoor water use drive up demand. Backflow assemblies on irrigation lines need annual testing. Water heaters work harder in summer, and hard water scale builds up faster.
    • Schedule your annual backflow test before the deadline. Flush your water heater if it has been more than a year since the last service.

  • Fall

    • Falling temperatures signal the start of frozen pipe season. Homeowners should disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots. Water heaters that struggled all summer may fail when the first cold snap hits.
    • Disconnect garden hoses and drain outdoor lines. Have your water heater inspected before winter. Insulate pipes in unheated spaces.

  • Winter

    • Frozen pipes are the top winter call in Hancock Square. Pipes in exterior walls and crawl spaces freeze and burst. Water heater failures spike during cold snaps. Emergency calls increase after prolonged freezes.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during cold snaps. If you travel, set the thermostat no lower than 55 degrees. Know where your main shut-off valve is.
