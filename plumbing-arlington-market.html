Common Plumbing Problems in Arlington Market

Arlington Market has a mix of newer buildings and older infrastructure, and that creates a specific set of plumbing problems. Here are the ones we see most often.

  • Root intrusion in older sewer lines

    The older surrounding infrastructure includes clay tile and cast iron sewer lines with joints that roots can find. Once roots get in, the line backs up regularly.

  • Hard water scaling in water heaters

    The water in this area is hard, which leaves scale in the tank and on the heating elements. That shortens the life of the water heater.

  • Grease clogs in commercial kitchen drains

    The market district has many restaurants. Grease builds up in the drain lines and narrows the pipe until it clogs.

  • Frozen pipes in exposed walls

    Older buildings sometimes have pipes in exterior walls. A cold snap can freeze them, and the pipe can burst when it thaws.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Arlington Market

The seasons change what we see in Arlington Market. Winter brings frozen pipes, spring brings sump pump calls, summer brings water heater strain, and fall is the time to prepare. Here is what to expect.

  • Spring

    • Spring rain raises the water table, and sump pumps run constantly. We see sump pump failure and sewer backup calls after heavy storms.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. If it does not kick on, call us.

  • Summer

    • Summer is hard on water heaters and outdoor plumbing. We see water heater failures and clogged drains from summer gatherings.
    • Flush your water heater once a year to remove sediment. It helps the unit run efficiently.

  • Fall

    • Fall is the time to prepare for winter. We see homeowners scheduling maintenance and repairing exterior faucets before the freeze.
    • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off exterior faucets before the first freeze.

  • Winter

    • Winter brings frozen pipes and burst lines. Older buildings with pipes in exterior walls are most at risk.
    • Keep your thermostat at 55 degrees or higher, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
