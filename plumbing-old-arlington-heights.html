Common Plumbing Problems in Old Arlington Heights

Old Arlington Heights has a specific set of plumbing problems that come from its age, its climate, and its water. The historic district's older homes still run on a lot of original piping, and Cook County's freeze-thaw cycles put stress on every line.

  • Galvanized steel supply lines rusting shut

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1950s, and it corrodes from the inside out. In Old Arlington Heights, many houses still have the original pipe, which narrows over decades until pressure drops and water runs brown.

  • Frozen pipes in exterior walls and crawl spaces

    Older homes in the historic district often have supply lines running through unheated exterior walls or crawl spaces. A sustained freeze can split the pipe, and the leak may not show until the ice thaws and water starts running.

  • Root intrusion in clay sewer laterals

    Mature parkway trees around the Vail Avenue area and Evergreen Avenue area send roots into the joints of old clay tile laterals. The roots grow into the pipe and catch debris, which causes repeated backups that snaking only temporarily clears.

  • Water heater failures from sediment and hard water

    Hard water leaves mineral scale on the bottom of the tank and on the anode rod. The scale insulates the burner, forces the unit to run longer, and shortens its life. In Old Arlington Heights, we see this most often in tanks that have never been flushed.

How Seasons Affect Plumbing in Old Arlington Heights

Plumbing problems in Old Arlington Heights follow the seasons. Winter brings frozen pipes and sump pump failures. Spring thaw sends water into basements and exposes leaks. Summer is peak season for water heater and drain work. Fall is the time to prepare before the first hard freeze.

  • Spring

    • Spring thaw sends meltwater into basements and crawl spaces, which is when sump pump failures show up. Sewer backups also increase as the ground shifts and roots start growing again.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit and confirming it kicks on and shuts off. If it runs constantly or not at all, call us before the next storm.

  • Summer

    • Summer is peak season for water heater replacements and drain cleaning. Hard water scale builds up faster in hot weather, and kitchen drains see more use.
    • Flush your water heater once a year to clear sediment. It takes about an hour and can add years to the tank's life.

  • Fall

    • Fall is the time to prepare for winter. Exterior faucets and irrigation lines need to be drained before the first freeze. Supply lines in unheated spaces should be insulated.
    • Disconnect garden hoses, shut off and drain exterior faucets, and insulate any exposed pipe in the garage, crawl space, or exterior walls.

  • Winter

    • Winter is our busiest season for emergency calls. Frozen pipes, burst supply lines, and failed water heaters are common. The freeze-thaw cycle in Cook County puts stress on every line, especially in older homes with pipes in exterior walls.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during cold snaps, and let a trickle of water run overnight. If a pipe does freeze, call us before you try to thaw it yourself.
