Common Plumbing Problems in Countryside

Countryside's climate, housing age, and tree canopy create specific plumbing challenges. Cold winters freeze pipes, mature trees invade sewer lines, and hard water scales up appliances. Knowing what to expect helps you prepare.

  • Frozen pipes in unheated crawl spaces

    Countryside winters regularly drop below freezing, and older homes often have uninsulated crawl spaces or garages where supply lines run. When water freezes, it expands and cracks the pipe, causing a leak when it thaws.

  • Tree roots blocking sewer laterals

    Countryside's mature trees, especially near Countryside Park, send roots into clay and cast iron sewer lines through joint gaps. Roots catch debris and create blockages that worsen over time.

  • Hard water scaling water heaters and fixtures

    Countryside's water supply contains dissolved minerals that build up inside water heaters and fixtures. Scale reduces efficiency, shortens appliance lifespan, and clogs faucets and showerheads.

  • Sump pump failures during spring storms

    Spring rains and snowmelt saturate the ground around Countryside basements. If your sump pump is old, clogged, or underpowered, it can fail during the storm when you need it most, leading to flooding.

Seasonal Plumbing Considerations in Countryside

Countryside's four seasons each bring different plumbing concerns. Winter freezes pipes, spring thaws saturate the ground, summer stresses water heaters, and fall is your window to prepare. If you need emergency drain cleaning Countryside or a seasonal tune-up, we're here year-round.

  • Spring

    • Spring rains and snowmelt saturate the soil around Countryside basements, putting pressure on sump pumps and foundation drains. Sewer lines can back up from excess groundwater. We see a spike in sump pump repairs and sewer cleaning calls.
    • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit. If it doesn't kick on, call us. Also schedule a sewer camera inspection if you had backups last year.

  • Summer

    • Summer is peak season for water heater stress, especially in homes with hard water. Scale builds up faster when the heater runs constantly.
    • Flush your water heater to remove sediment. If you're remodeling, call us early to plan the plumbing rough-in before walls go up.

  • Fall

    • Fall is the time to prepare for winter. We winterize exposed pipes, insulate crawl spaces, and check shut-off valves. Leaves and debris can clog gutters and exterior drains, leading to water intrusion.
    • Disconnect garden hoses, shut off exterior spigots, and insulate pipes in unheated areas. Schedule a maintenance visit before the first freeze.

  • Winter

    • Winter brings frozen pipes, burst lines, and water heater failures. Countryside's cold snaps can freeze exposed pipes in crawl spaces and garages. We get emergency calls for no heat, no water, and leaking pipes.
    • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks during extreme cold to let warm air circulate. If a pipe freezes, thaw it gently with a hair dryer, not an open flame. Call us if you see cracks or leaks.
